# Atlassian Rovo

This one isn't part of today's workshop exercises, but it's worth its own guide: per our recent
questionnaire, Rovo is the AI tool a substantial number of functional analysts already work with day to day, since
it's built into Jira and Confluence. Everything the workshop practices, applies directly to how you should use Rovo (or any other AI tool).

## What Rovo is

Rovo is Atlassian's AI layer across the tools you already use: Jira, Confluence, and other
connected apps. Rather than being a separate destination you go to, it's built into the tools
you're already working in.

## The three parts worth knowing

**Rovo Search.** A natural-language search across your connected tools (Jira, Confluence, and
often other integrated systems). Instead of trying to guess the right keywords for a page
title, you can ask a full question, "what did we decide about the extension approval process
for our application?", and it searches across everything it has access to, not just titles.

**Rovo Chat.** A conversational assistant, similar in spirit to any AI chat tool, but aware of
your Jira and Confluence content when you ask it to be. You can ask it to summarize a thread of
comments, explain a ticket's history, or draft text based on existing pages.

**Rovo Agents.** Pre-built, purpose-specific assistants that do more structured, multi-step
work. One relevant example is the **Product Requirements Expert** agent, which can search
across Confluence and Jira, read linked URLs, filter and search Jira issues, and draft
Confluence pages or comments based on what it finds. This is closer to what our "agent
mode" exercise demonstrates, just built specifically for requirements and product work inside
Atlassian's tools.

## Getting started

Rovo is typically already available if your organization has it enabled, look for a Rovo icon
or a "Ask Rovo" entry point inside Jira or Confluence. If you don't see it, check with your
Atlassian admin, it may need to be enabled for your site or your specific license tier.

Once available:

1. Try Rovo Search first: type a real question you'd normally have to dig for the answer to.
2. Try Rovo Chat on something you already have in Confluence: ask it to summarize a long page
   or a comment thread.
3. If a Product Requirements Expert agent is available in your instance, try pointing it at a
   real (non-sensitive) requirements question and see what it produces.

## Applying today's habits to Rovo

Everything you practice in the workshop transfers directly:

- **Check what it's grounded in.** Rovo Chat and Rovo Agents draw from your actual Jira/
  Confluence content when relevant, but always check its answer against the source page it's
  citing, the same way you'd check an AI's answer against the actual brief in this workshop.
- **Watch for silently resolved contradictions.** If two stakeholders left conflicting comments
  on a ticket, don't assume Rovo will flag that conflict for you. Ask directly.
- **Treat generated drafts as a first pass.** A Confluence page or comment Rovo drafts for you
  is a strong starting point, not a finished, verified answer.
